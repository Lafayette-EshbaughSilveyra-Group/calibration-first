# Cross-Domain Validation of Calibration-First Supervision Construction

## Objective

The purpose of this experiment was to evaluate whether the proposed calibration-first fusion framework produces higher-quality supervision signals than either (1) naive averaging of heterogeneous sources or (2) any individual source alone. Importantly, ground-truth labels were withheld during supervision construction and used only for external validation.

This experiment was designed to test the central claim of *Align Before You Combine*: before heterogeneous signals can be meaningfully fused, they should first be aligned to a common reference space.

---

## Experimental Design

Two benchmark datasets from unrelated domains were selected:

1. **Ames Housing** (regression)
   - Target withheld during supervision construction: `SalePrice`
   - Goal: determine whether calibration-first fusion produces a ranking that aligns with actual housing value.

2. **Breast Cancer Wisconsin** (classification)
   - Target withheld during supervision construction: malignant vs. benign diagnosis.
   - Goal: determine whether calibration-first fusion produces a ranking that aligns with true malignancy risk.

In both cases, labels were completely excluded from the construction of the fused supervision signal and were used only after the fact for evaluation.

---

## Methodology

### Step 1: Construct Independent Scoring Functions

For each dataset, several domain-inspired scoring functions were defined. These scoring functions acted as independent information sources.

For Ames Housing, the sources measured:

- Physical scale of the home
- Construction quality and condition
- Age and modernization
- Garage capacity

For Breast Cancer Wisconsin, the sources measured:

- Tumor size
- Shape irregularity
- Texture and smoothness
- Worst-case measurements

Each source produced a raw score intended to capture one aspect of an underlying latent concept (house value or malignancy risk).

---

### Step 2: Construct a Synthetic Calibration Space

Rather than calibrating against ground-truth labels, a synthetic ordinal calibration space was constructed.

For Ames Housing, synthetic homes were generated by enumerating combinations of:

- Living area
- Quality level
- Modernization level
- Garage capacity

For Breast Cancer Wisconsin, synthetic tumors were generated by enumerating combinations of:

- Size
- Shape characteristics
- Texture characteristics
- Worst-case measurements

Each calibration space consisted of a structured grid of hypothetical examples spanning low to high values of the latent construct.

---

### Step 3: Calibration

Each scoring function was applied to the synthetic calibration space.

The resulting score distribution provided a reference frame for that modality. Raw scores observed in the real dataset were standardized relative to the synthetic reference distribution using z-score normalization.

This step transformed heterogeneous scoring functions into a common calibrated space.

---

### Step 4: Fusion

After calibration, the standardized modality scores were averaged:

$$
\kappa = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^{m} z_i
$$

where each $z_i$ represents a modality calibrated against the synthetic reference space.

This produced the fused supervision signal $\kappa$.

---

### Baselines

The calibrated fusion score was compared against:

1. **Uncalibrated Average**
   - Direct averaging of raw modality scores.

2. **Single-Source Models**
   - Each modality evaluated individually.

This comparison isolates the effect of calibration.

---

## Results

### Ames Housing

| Method                       | Spearman ρ |
|------------------------------|------------|
| Calibrated Fusion ($\kappa$) | **0.8905** |
| Uncalibrated Average         | 0.8593     |
| Best Single Source           | 0.8150     |

Additional metrics:

- Kendall $\tau$: **0.7197**
- Top 10% overlap with true house prices: **73.97%**

The calibrated fusion score achieved the strongest agreement with actual housing prices despite never observing the `SalePrice` target during construction.

Spearman's rank correlation measures the degree to which two rankings agree. A value of 1.0 indicates perfect agreement, while a value of 0 indicates no monotonic relationship. The observed Spearman correlation of 0.8905 indicates that the ordering induced by the calibrated supervision signal closely matches the ordering induced by actual market prices.

Similarly, Kendall's $\tau$ measures agreement between pairs of observations and provides an alternative rank-based assessment that is often more conservative. The observed value of 0.7197 indicates strong agreement between the pairwise ordering implied by $\kappa$ and the pairwise ordering implied by true house prices.

The top-10% overlap metric evaluates whether the highest-scoring properties identified by the supervision signal correspond to the most valuable properties in the dataset. A value of 73.97% means that nearly three-quarters of the properties ranked in the top decile by $\kappa$ also appear in the true top decile by sale price.

---

### Breast Cancer Wisconsin

| Method                       | AUROC      |
|------------------------------|------------|
| Calibrated Fusion ($\kappa$) | **0.9843** |
| Uncalibrated Average         | 0.9575     |
| Best Single Source           | 0.9705     |

Additional metrics:

- Average Precision: **0.9770**
- Spearman correlation with malignancy status: **0.8111**

Again, calibrated fusion outperformed both naive fusion and every individual source.

AUROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve) measures the ability of a scoring function to rank positive examples above negative examples across all possible classification thresholds. An AUROC of 0.9843 indicates that if a malignant tumor and a benign tumor are selected at random, the calibrated fusion score will assign a higher risk score to the malignant tumor approximately 98.4% of the time.

Average Precision evaluates how effectively positive examples are concentrated near the top of the ranking. The observed value of 0.9770 indicates that malignant cases are consistently assigned higher scores than benign cases and tend to appear near the top of the induced ranking.

The Spearman correlation of 0.8111 provides an additional perspective by measuring agreement between the continuous supervision signal and the binary diagnostic outcome. Together, these metrics suggest that calibration-first fusion produces a supervision signal that not only separates the two classes effectively but also captures a meaningful ordering of malignancy risk despite never observing diagnostic labels during construction.

---

## Interpretation

The key result is not simply that $\kappa$ achieved high predictive performance.

Rather, the important finding is that calibration consistently improved fusion quality.

Across both domains:

- Calibrated fusion outperformed naive averaging.
- Calibrated fusion outperformed every individual source.
- Labels were never used during supervision construction.

These findings support the hypothesis that heterogeneous scoring functions benefit from alignment to a shared reference space before aggregation.

---

## What This Experiment Demonstrates

This experiment provides evidence that calibration-first supervision construction is not limited to the synthetic-home setting used in the primary case study.

The same procedure successfully transferred to:

- Real-estate valuation
- Medical risk assessment

without modification of the underlying framework.

More broadly, the results suggest that synthetic calibration spaces can serve as useful reference structures for aligning heterogeneous supervision sources, allowing meaningful fusion even when ground-truth labels are unavailable.

This supports the central claim of *Align Before You Combine*: calibration is not merely a preprocessing step, but a mechanism that enables more effective supervision construction from multiple imperfect information sources.
